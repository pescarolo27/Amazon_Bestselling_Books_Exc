Amazon Bestselling Books (Excel)

**The Data:** The dataset, sourced from ([Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/sootersaalu/amazon-top-50-bestselling-books-2009-2019)), is built from the top-50 bestselling books on Amazon each year between 2009-2019. It contains information including the book title, author, average Amazon rating, number of Amazon reviews, price, when it was a bestseller, & the genre. The raw version of the data is available in the "orig_data" sheet in the Excel file. Since there are 50 books across 11 years, there are 550 data points.  
By analyzing this data, valuable insights including patterns & trends can be obtained that can inform authors, publishers, & readers what book characteristics have been the most popular in recent years according to Amazon.

This project was done in May, 2025. In the Excel file, there are six sheets containing the following: notes on the dataset, the raw dataset, a cleaner, slightly modified version of the data, a non-duplicated version of the data, analyses including pivot tables & visuals, & a dashboard containing important metrics & figures ("Dashboard (1)").

### Brief Summary
This project examined information on the bestselling books on Amazon between 2009-2019. Various metrics & figures were assembled including the average user rating, the number of reoccuring books, the books & authors with the most appearances on the bestsellers list, the books with the most ratings & reviews, the average yearly prices, & more.

The original version of the dataset required no preprocessing; however, a book-index variable was created to keep unique track of each book & the year it was on the bestsellers list. There were 350 unique books that made this list over these 11 years.

In terms of other key metrics, there were 248 authors who made the list & over 6,500,000 Amazon reviews of these books during this eleven-year period. The average user rating across these books was about 4.62. 95 of the 350 books made the list more than once. There were 160 & 190 fiction & non-fiction books respectively. Most were less than $25, but the average price of these books was usually between $10-$15 each year. There were only 19 books with an average user rating of 4.0 (out of 5) or less.

Eight books made the list at least six times, two of which were present in ten of the eleven years. Of books that are not textbooks, manuals, or guides, "The Very Hungry Caterpillar" & "Oh, the Places You'll Go!" are among these eight. These two titles were among the books with the highest average ratings (4.9/5 each).  
Eight authors were on the list at least eight times. Jeff Kinney, who had 12 unique books make the list, was included 12 times. Suzanne Collins & Rick Riordan, who had five & ten unique books make the list respectively, were included 11 times each.
